How the System Works: Data Structures & Algorithms (DSA) Engine Academic Project • CCSE0301 (DSA-II)

Module 1 & 2 Graph Theory & Graph Coloring

Problem: In modern universities, students take different core and elective subjects across multiple departments. If an exam schedule is made manually, students frequently get scheduled for two exams simultaneously.

Solution: We model courses as Vertices and shared student clashes as Edges in an undirected graph. Assigning non-clashing time slots maps mathematically to Vertex Graph Coloring: adjacent vertices can never share the same color (exam day).

Module 1 Priority Queues & Binary Max-Heaps

DSATUR Optimization: To minimize the required exam days to exactly 6, the algorithm dynamically picks the most constrained exam—the one whose neighbors already use the highest variety of colors (saturation degree).

A Binary Max-Heap Priority Queue with an array-based reverse lookup map updates these priorities in O(log V) time, guaranteeing instant sub-millisecond computation.

Module 3 Dynamic Programming (Room Packing & Bench Pairing)

Classroom Seating & Companion Pairing: Once days are determined, physical rooms must be assigned without overcrowding.

We apply the 0/1 Knapsack Bellman Recurrence (DP[i, w] = max(DP[i-1, w], DP[i-1, w - w_i] + v_i)) to pack student cohorts into classrooms and interleave alternating benches between non-conflicting departments to prevent cheating.

Module 4 Backtracking & Branch and Bound

Mathematical Optimality: An exact recursive Depth-First Search explores candidate schedules, pruning immediately when a conflict is encountered.

We prove timetable optimality by calculating the Maximum Clique Lower Bound (χ(G) ≥ ω(G) = 6). Because the largest mutually conflicting cluster requires 6 slots, 6 days is mathematically proven optimal.
